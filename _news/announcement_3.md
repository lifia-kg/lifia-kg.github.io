## Jornada de Investigadores en Formación – Universidad Nacional de Quilmes (JIF-UNQUI)

### Overview

Members of the LIFIA Knowledge-Graph Team participated in the Jornada de Investigadores en Formación held at the Universidad Nacional de Quilmes (UNQ), where part of the ongoing work within the Land Value Observatory (OVS) project was presented.
The event highlighted research advances in knowledge graphs, natural-language processing, and geospatial analysis applied to the real-estate domain.

Three presentations were delivered, each addressing a different technical aspect of the project.

### Presentations

## 1. Visualizing Knowledge Graphs with Geospatial Data for Real-Estate Market Analysis

This presentation introduced a novel approach for visualizing knowledge graphs enriched with geospatial information, enabling improved exploration and understanding of real-estate datasets within the OVS framework.

The work showcased techniques for:

Integrating map-based visualizations with semantic graph structures

Improving readability and navigation of complex relationships

Supporting territorial analysis through enhanced spatial context

This visualization strategy contributes to more intuitive interactions with real-estate knowledge graphs.

## 2. Natural-Language Processing for Real-Estate Attribute Extraction — OVS

This talk focused on the development of natural-language processing (NLP) methods for automatically detecting and extracting property attributes from unstructured real-estate descriptions.

Topics covered included:

Attribute extraction from property listings

Integration of NLP outputs into the OVS knowledge graph

Improving data completeness and semantic richness

Challenges in handling noisy, ambiguous, or incomplete real-world listings

The results support automated enrichment of the OVS data pipeline.

## 3. Answering Spatio-Temporal Questions in Dynamic Knowledge Graphs

The final presentation explored techniques for handling spatio-temporal queries over dynamic knowledge graphs, where property information evolves over time.

The work addressed:

Representing temporal changes within the knowledge graph

Querying spatial and temporal dimensions simultaneously

Supporting analysis of trends, market evolution, and property transformations

Adapting knowledge-graph structures to dynamic real-estate data

This line of research aims to enable more expressive analytical capabilities within the OVS ecosystem.
